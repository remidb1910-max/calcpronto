How it works

French legal severance pay is 1/4 of a month of salary per year of seniority up to 10 years, then 1/3 of a month per year beyond; an incomplete year counts pro rata by complete months. It is due after 8 months of uninterrupted service, except for gross or serious misconduct. The indemnity of a rupture conventionnelle (mutual termination of a permanent contract) cannot be lower: the minimum is the same, and your collective agreement may give more.

The reference salary is the more favourable of two amounts: the gross monthly average of the last 12 months (of all months if fewer), or that of the last 3 months, an annual bonus paid in those 3 months counting for 3/12 only. Seniority runs to the end of the contract, notice included; non-occupational sick leave, unpaid or sabbatical leave and strike days do not count.

For income tax, the indemnity is exempt up to the highest of the legal or conventional amount, twice your gross annual pay of the previous year or half the indemnity (the last two capped at €288,360 in 2026). The tax-exempt part is free of social contributions up to €96,120 (2 social security ceilings); above €480,600 everything is subject. CSG (9.2%) and CRDS (0.5%) apply to what exceeds the legal or conventional minimum, on 100% of it.

In practice, an indemnity equal to the legal minimum is paid free of tax, contributions and CSG-CRDS. What you negotiate on top bears 9.7% of CSG-CRDS. For a rupture conventionnelle, the employer pays a 40% contribution on the part excluded from contributions (30% before 2026). An employee already entitled to a basic pension is taxed from the first euro on a rupture conventionnelle indemnity.

Example

An employee paid €2,500 gross a month for 12 years and 6 months is entitled to at least 2,500 × 1/4 × 10 = €6,250, plus 2,500 × 1/3 × 2.5 = €2,083.33, so €8,333.33, free of tax and CSG. In a rupture conventionnelle, if €12,000 is agreed, the €3,666.67 above the minimum bears €337.33 of CSG and €18.33 of CRDS: the employee receives €11,644.34, with no income tax. The employer also pays €4,800 of employer contribution (40%).

Frequently asked questions

Is the rupture conventionnelle indemnity the same as dismissal pay?

Its minimum is: it cannot be lower than the legal dismissal indemnity (or the conventional one if your collective agreement gives more). Anything above is negotiated. Tax and contribution rules are almost the same; the differences: the employer pays a 40% contribution on a rupture conventionnelle, and an employee already entitled to a pension is taxed from the first euro.

Is French severance pay taxable?

Not as long as it stays below the highest of: the legal or conventional minimum, twice your gross pay of the previous year, or half the indemnity (at most €288,360 in 2026). Most negotiated indemnities are therefore tax-free. The part above the minimum does bear 9.7% of CSG-CRDS, withheld by the employer.

How is seniority counted?

Up to the end of the contract: the end of notice for a dismissal, the termination date set in the agreement for a rupture conventionnelle. Months beyond full years count pro rata. Maternity leave, work accidents and paid holidays count; non-occupational sick leave, unpaid or sabbatical leave and strikes do not.

What if I have less than 8 months of seniority?

After a dismissal, no legal severance pay is due (your collective agreement may provide one). In a rupture conventionnelle the indemnity is due whatever the seniority according to service-public.fr, but no text sets its minimum: the calculator then asks for the agreed amount.

What does the employer pay on top?

For a rupture conventionnelle, a contribution of 40% of the part of the indemnity excluded from social contributions, since 1 January 2026 (30% before). For a €10,000 indemnity that is €4,000. For a dismissal, nothing more as long as the indemnity is exempt from contributions.
