Jet2 delay and cancellation compensation

A Jet2 flight arriving 3 hours late or more entitles you to £350 per passenger, for example on a Manchester – Palma de Mallorca flight (1,581 km) under UK261, unless there were extraordinary circumstances. From the EU, the EU regulation also applies: €400 on a Palma de Mallorca – Manchester flight (1,581 km).

Which Jet2 flights are covered

Operating licence: Jet2.com (United Kingdom).

Since Brexit a British airline is no longer an EU airline: the EU regulation covers it only on departures from the EU. UK261 covers its flights from the UK and its flights to the UK or to the EU.

Between two airports of the EU, the EEA or Switzerland, the EU regulation applies whatever the airline.

From the departure delay shown in the table, Jet2 must also provide meals and drinks, and a hotel if departure is moved to the next day; from 5 hours of delay you may give up the trip and ask for a refund of the ticket.

Cancellation: compensation is due if you were told less than 14 days before departure, unless you were offered another flight close to the original times (told 7 to 13 days before: departing no more than 2 hours earlier and arriving less than 4 hours later; told less than 7 days before: departing no more than 1 hour earlier and arriving less than 2 hours later).

Denied boarding against your will (overbooking): immediate compensation, same amount, with no extraordinary-circumstances defence.

When Jet2 can refuse to pay

The airline does not owe compensation if it proves extraordinary circumstances it could not avoid: weather unsuitable for the flight, air traffic control decisions, strikes outside the airline (air traffic controllers, airport staff), security risks, a bird strike (CJEU, Pešková, C-315/15).

Ordinary technical problems with the aircraft (CJEU, Wallentin-Hermann, C-549/07) and strikes by the airline's own staff (CJEU, Krüsemann, C-195/17; Airhelp v SAS, C-28/20) generally are not. Meals, drinks and hotel are owed in every case.

Frequently asked questions

How much does Jet2 have to pay for a flight more than 3 hours late?

£350 per passenger, for example on a Manchester – Palma de Mallorca flight (1,581 km). The amount depends on the flight distance, not on the ticket price.

Is a Jet2 flight to Europe from abroad covered?

Not by the EU regulation: since Brexit Jet2 is no longer an EU airline. UK261, however, covers its flights to the UK and to the EU (for example Antalya – Manchester: £350).

My Jet2 flight is operated by another airline: who pays?

The airline that actually operates the flight (“operated by” on the booking or boarding pass), even if Jet2 sold the ticket. For a flight to Europe from a non-EU country, its licence decides whether the EU regulation applies.

How long do I have to claim?

It depends on the country where you claim: six years in England and Wales, for example. Claim in writing to the airline first, then escalate to the national enforcement body or an approved dispute scheme.
